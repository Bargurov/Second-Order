and 8 now name time, prerequisites and limits explicitly, and the table at the end says what the new checks catch.
The same day (econ-calc-0.5, `docs/news-flow-lengths-2026-10-29.md` (private; not in the public repository)), criterion 8 and the table add that flow totals
are compared only over a length of time their kind establishes. On 1 November (econ-calc-0.6,
`docs/news-space-thousands-2026-11-01.md` (private; not in the public repository)) the table adds how numbers whose thousands are grouped by spaces are read,
and on 4 November (econ-calc-0.7, `docs/news-number-boundaries-2026-11-04.md` (private; not in the public repository)) how a grouped number after a year, a
range and a run of digit groups that could be one number are read.

A story's shared economic explanation (`second_order/story_economics.py`) passes three kinds of automated check before
it can be published: its **shape** (every field, count and reference resolves), its **evidence boundary** (figures are
quoted from the story's material or its context bundle, derived figures are computed in code, a mechanism note must
apply to the development's instrument, each channel states what supports it, a second-order effect must follow from a
channel and state its condition, a figure keeps the sign its quote states, and a calculation is refused when its inputs
are different summary statistics, different kinds of quantity or bounds that give no bound) and a **lexical and
numeric guard** (econ-guard-0.2: unsourced numbers, unbenchmarked surprises, "real" readings of nominal figures, levels
presented as changes, unsupported comparisons with the past, companies the evidence does not name, security price
forecasts, advice, causal and certain claims, and a figure whose direction its words contradict or do not establish).

None of these checks establishes that an explanation is economically right. They catch a defined set of mistakes in
words, numbers and references. Whether the mechanism is the right one, whether the steps follow, whether the effects
are plausible in size and timing, and whether the alternatives are the serious ones are judgements a person makes with
this rubric. An explanation published without such a review is labelled "AI-assisted · not read by an editor" (or
"AI-drafted"), and the reader is told so; a review is recorded only when someone states that they read the version
(`news batch publish --reviewed`, or the desk's review action).

## How to review

Read the story's sources first (the Sources section and each publisher's page), then the explanation's first layer,
then each expandable layer. For each criterion below, record **meets**, **partly** or **fails**, with one sentence of
reason. An explanation that fails any of criteria 1 to 5 should be withdrawn or replaced by a new version; one that is
"partly" on several should be revised. A reviewer who finds the evidence too thin should prefer a new version that
says so (`status: insufficient_evidence`) over a weaker explanation.

| # | Criterion | Meets when | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What changed, classified correctly** | The stage (proposal, decision, implementation, observation, forecast) and the instrument match the sources; a proposal or a forecast is never written up as if in force. | An announced measure treated as implemented; a domestic service's "tariff" (a price) classified as an import duty. |
| 2 | **Facts and figures traceable** | Every factual statement rests on a cited report, fact, figure or context item; each figure keeps its unit, period, nominal or real basis, seasonal adjustment and vintage. | A number from memory; a figure quoted without its period; an advance estimate presented as final. |
| 3 | **Economic significance proportionate** | Why it matters follows from what the sources state about scope and size; magnitude context comes only from the evidence. | A small or local measure presented as economy-wide; significance asserted with no basis. |
| 4 | **Right mechanism for the instrument** | Each channel is a mechanism that applies to this kind of development (a framework note only where it applies); macro and micro channels appear only where the evidence supports them. | The tariff pass-through mechanism used for a domestic price; a monetary transmission story told about a statistical release. |
| 4a | **Support stated and real** | Each channel says what supports it: a **framework** note that covers this mechanism (not one attached to fill the field); **event evidence**, where a source states the mechanism and the explanation attributes it; or a **bounded inference** whose assumptions and conditions are written out. The cited note or source actually supports the step it is cited for. | A note about trade costs cited for a payments story; a speaker's claim presented as the explanation's own finding; an inference with no stated assumption. |
| 5 | **Steps and affected parties follow** | The intermediate steps connect the change to the affected firms, industries, households or institutions without leaps; named companies appear in the evidence. The steps are the links of one mechanism, not a timeline: a prerequisite (an adoption, a decision, a finding) is a condition, a separately dated plan is a fact, and an order in time between two developments is stated only where the evidence states it. That A requires B says nothing of when A happens relative to C. | "Prices rise → recession"; a named company that no source mentions; a pilot dated 2027 placed after an issuance decision that depends only on the legislation. |
| 6 | **Second-order effects earned** | Each follows from a stated channel, with its condition and a defensible horizon; there are none rather than invented ones. | An effect two steps removed with no condition; a "global" effect from a facility-level matter. |
| 7 | **Alternatives and offsetting forces serious** | The alternatives are the ones an economist would raise first (absorption by exporters, substitution, policy responses, measurement noise). Each says what supports it (its sources, or an inference whose assumptions are written out), as a channel does. | A token alternative; none where an obvious offsetting force exists; an offsetting force asserted with no mechanism, or resting on a source that says the opposite. |
| 8 | **Measurement discipline** | Nominal is not called real; levels are not changes; growth rates differ in percentage points; adjusted and unadjusted figures are not compared; revisions and vintages are acknowledged. A figure keeps its direction ("fell by 4%" is −4); a bound stays a bound ("more than 95%" is at least 95, and so is what is computed from it); summary statistics of different survey questions (a mean, a median) are not combined into a statistic of something else; a stock is set against a flow as periods of that flow, never as a share of it; a limit on a stock (holdings, a balance, a capacity) is not a limit on a flow (payments, spending, sales, lending), and an effect of a limit on behaviour is an inference with its mechanism; a flow over a half-year or a day is not a year's flow, while any twelve consecutive months are; two flow totals compare only over the same length of time, whatever their dates, and the time between two figures is not the length of either; the methodology (seasonal adjustment, price adjustment, confidence intervals, significance) is checked against the publisher's own release. | "Real sales rose" from a nominal series; "growth rose 25%" from 2.0% to 2.5%; mean expected nominal income deflated by median expected inflation and called expected real income; end-of-month inventories shown as "132.7% of the month's sales"; a change inside its confidence interval read as a fall; a cap on wallet holdings read as a cap on how much is used; a half-year deficit recorded as a level of no kind beside a year's record; a one-day total compared with a one-year total; "from $2.5 billion to $7.5 billion in twelve months" read as two twelve-month totals. |
| 9 | **Expectations and history evidenced** | A release is a surprise only against a contemporaneous expectations benchmark in the context bundle; comparisons with the past come from the evidence. | "Beat expectations" with no benchmark; "the highest since 2019" from memory. |
| 10 | **Uncertainty and observables useful** | The main uncertainty is the one that most changes the reading; the observables would actually support or weaken it. | A generic uncertainty; observables that cannot distinguish the reading from its alternatives. |
| 11 | **Language** | Conditional, attributed where a cause is someone's claim; no price forecasts for securities; no advice. | "This will cause…"; "steel shares will fall"; "investors should…". |
| 12 | **Honest limits** | What was read is stated (headlines and excerpts, or full text); an explanation from headlines alone is modest or says the evidence is insufficient. | A confident mechanism built on a headline. |
| 13 | **One coherent page** | The brief's summary, facts, uncertainties and missing items agree with the explanation: an unknown the context answered is no longer listed as unknown; what was read is said precisely (quoted passages of a release are not its full text; a methodology note is not the news). | A summary saying a mark's meaning "is not supplied" above an explanation that gives it. |
| 14 | **Context in its place** | Each context item is relevant for a stated reason, has its role (evidence on this development, a comparison, methodology, a benchmark) and its dates; later context is shown as later and never as what was known at the time; no item is attached to make an explanation look better supported. | A revision published a week later cited as what the release said; an unrelated background page cited for a figure. |
| 15 | **Useful beyond the headline** | The explanation names the meaningful development, a fitting mechanism and its steps, the material assumptions and serious alternatives, and observations that would tell the readings apart; where it cannot, it says the evidence is insufficient, with the specific reason. | A paraphrase of the headline with a generic mechanism; a forced macro and micro channel. |

## What the automated checks catch, and what they miss

| Mistake | Caught by | Not caught |
|---|---|---|
| A domestic price read through the import-tariff mechanism | Refused when a channel names a framework note that does not apply to the declared instrument; flagged when "import_tariff" is declared and the evidence never speaks of imports or duties. | A channel with no framework note that describes import pass-through in its own words. |
| Nominal called real | Flagged when a sentence says "real" (or "inflation-adjusted") and cites a nominal figure or calculation; a calculation combining nominal and real figures is refused. | "Purchasing power fell" written about a nominal figure without the word "real". |
| Level presented as change | Flagged when "rose/fell by {f}" cites a level. | A level described as a change in other words. |
| Percent change of growth rates | Refused by the calculation (levels only; growth rates differ in percentage points). | A hand-written comparison in words (numbers outside placeholders are flagged as unsourced only if they are not in the evidence). |
| Unsupported expectations | Flagged for "surprise", "than expected", "consensus" and similar words without a cited expectations benchmark dated before the release. | Expectations implied without those words. |
| Unjustified second-order effects | Refused without a source channel or condition; flagged without a hedge. | An effect that is formally connected but economically implausible. |
| Invented numbers, comparisons, companies | Flagged when a number is not in the evidence, a comparison with the past cites no evidence, or a named firm's words are not in the evidence. | Spelled-out numbers ("forty percent"); a real company name that also appears in the evidence for another reason. |
| A figure with the wrong sign | Refused when the quote states the opposite sign (ASCII or Unicode minus, plus); refused when the cited direction words ("fell by") contradict the value's sign; flagged when an unsigned change is positive but the words near it state a decline, or when the cited words do not establish a direction. Ranges ("2021–2025") and margins ("±0.4") are not read as signs. | A direction stated far from the number, or only in another sentence. |
| Bounds as point estimates | A figure records whether it is exact, a lower or upper bound, or approximate; calculations carry the bound ("at least +3 percentage points") and refuse combinations with no bound (one lower bound minus another). | A bound the drafter records as exact although its quote says "more than" (a reviewer compares the quote). |
| Mixed statistics, stocks and flows | Refused: deflating anything but observed values; a stock set against a flow without both kinds stated; a share outside zero to its whole; any calculation whose inputs differ in statistic or kind. | A figure whose statistic or kind the drafter records wrongly (a reviewer checks the release's methodology). |
| A channel without support | Refused: framework support without a note, event evidence without a cited fact, report or context item, a bounded inference without assumptions and conditions. | A note or source that is cited but does not support the step (a reviewer reads it). |
| Changed background | A version keeps the exact framework notes it used, with their digests; a historical read shows them, and a note revised or removed since makes the explanation outdated, with the reason. | – |
| Later context as what happened | Flagged (econ-guard-0.3, `retrospective_as_event`) when what changed cites, or rests on a figure or calculation from, a context item published after the story was first reported; such items are labelled wherever shown. | Later knowledge written into other parts of the explanation in words. |
| Context without a reason or a role | Refused: a supplied item without its role, relevance, dates or terms; a role that does not fit its kind; methodology or an earlier release offered only when the feed and release title match. | An item whose stated reason does not hold (a reviewer reads it). |
| Newer context left unused | A bundle built after an explanation was prepared makes it outdated until it is prepared again. | – |
| An order in time the evidence does not state (0.6) | Nothing automatic: the request contracts say that steps are not a timeline, that a requirement relates only what it names and that an unstated relationship stays unstated. A lexical rule was rejected: most ordering words on current pages are correct. | Any order of events, a dated plan placed among a mechanism's steps, "before" or "after" between unrelated developments. |
| A limit read as limiting another quantity (0.6) | Refused: a calculation combining a stock with a flow other than as periods of a month, quarter or year, and a flow over another or an unstated period (econ-calc-0.4) combined with a level of another kind. | The same reading in prose, with or without a figure (a reviewer reads what the source says the limit applies to). |
| Flows of different or unknown lengths compared (econ-calc-0.5) | Refused: a difference, percent change or share of two flows over another or an unstated period, whose kind establishes no length (their figures stay quoted); a stock read as periods of such a flow (0.4). | A flow typed with the wrong kind (a day's total typed as a year's, a flow typed as no kind); a comparison written in words; months or quarters of unequal length, taken as equal. |
| An alternative without support (0.6) | Refused: an alternative that does not say what supports it, an inference that names no assumption, and stated evidence that cites no fact, report or context item. | A cited source that does not state the mechanism, an assumption the evidence contradicts, a weak alternative with valid support. |
| Thousands grouped by spaces (econ-calc-0.6) | Read as one number by every check (“350 000” is 350,000, with a space, no-break, narrow no-break or thin space), so a figure holding a fraction of it is refused; telephone-style runs of digit groups are not joined. | Separate numbers written in a row with single spaces (a table row such as “12 345 678”) read as one; a grouped number written straight after another number stays apart (from econ-calc-0.7: read whole after a year, and never a figure when it stays apart; next row). |
| Digit groups after a year, in a range or in an unjoined run (econ-calc-0.7) | “In 2025 350 000” is 2025 and 350,000, and “350 000-400 000” is a range of 350,000 to 400,000, in every check. A figure is never read from a run of digit groups that could also be one number and is not joined (“+32 2 295 75 01”, “350 000 12”), and a figure the quote writes only as one end of a range written with a dash is a bound (lower_bound for the lower end of a positive value), never an exact or approximate point. | A range written in words (“between 3% and 4%”) is not read as a range; the stops for a number the evidence does not state still count the separate groups of an unjoined run as stated numbers; two grouped numbers joined by a hyphen inside a telephone number would read as a range. |

Passing every check means only that none of these patterns was found. The demonstration of 2026-10-26
(`docs/news-economics-2026-10-26.md` (private; not in the public repository)) lists what the preparer's own review found; the AI economic review of the same
explanations, with the corrected versions it published, is in `docs/news-economics-review-2026-10-26.md` (private; not in the public repository).
